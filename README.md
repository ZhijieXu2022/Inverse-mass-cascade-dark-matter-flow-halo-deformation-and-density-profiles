# Inverse mass cascade in dark matter flow and effects on halo deformation, energy, size, and density profiles

Xu Z., 2026, *Inverse mass cascade in dark matter flow and effects on halo deformation, energy, size, and density profiles*, Zenodo [doi:10.5281/zenodo.24095562](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24095562). The preprint is hosted on arXiv as [arXiv:2109.12244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12244); 

## Major findings

- If a halo grows self-similarly, with mass and radius both proportional to time at fixed concentration c, mass conservation fixes the mean radial velocity inside it for any density profile: outward in the core and inward beyond about twice the scale radius. For an NFW halo the net radial momentum vanishes at c<sub>0</sub> = 3.48.
- Growth adds surface and kinetic terms to the virial balance. At c<sub>0</sub> they give an effective virial exponent n<sub>e</sub> = −1.42 instead of −1.
- A halo radius that grows by random accretion events follows geometric Brownian motion. For a simple osmotic flow the particle Fokker–Planck solution is a generalized gamma profile that contains the Einasto profile as a special case.
- Tested on composite haloes (all haloes with the same particle number, 4–6326 particles) of a 256<sup>3</sup> SCDM simulation, the generalized gamma profile fits the cumulative mass profiles with a median rms residual of 0.006 dex and a<sub>r</sub>/b<sub>r</sub> = 1.41 ± 0.05; the flat inner part lies within about one softening length.
- The measured mean radial velocity is inward at all resolved radii, between −0.39 H r and −1.24 H r, where self-similar growth predicts about +0.53 H r at the scale radius.
- The radial velocity dispersion exceeds the isotropic Jeans value by 1.11–1.90 times. Self-similar growth describes the profile shapes, but not the instantaneous kinematics, of these haloes.
